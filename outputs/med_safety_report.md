### **Clinical Safety Summary: Medication Regimen Review**

**Summary of Key Findings:**
This medication regimen presents significant and interacting risks, primarily centered on renal safety. The concurrent use of lisinopril (an ACE inhibitor) and diclofenac (an NSAID) is well-documented to impair renal autoregulation, substantially increasing the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI). This risk is compounded in a patient with diabetes, which is a risk factor for underlying renal disease. The resultant decline in renal function then elevates the dual risks of hyperkalemia from lisinopril and lactic acidosis from metformin accumulation. The patient's current renal function (eGFR) is unknown, which is a critical gap for safe management.

**Key Identified Risks:**
1.  **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** High risk due to the pharmacodynamic interaction between lisinopril and diclofenac. This risk is heightened by potential volume depletion (e.g., from illness or poor intake) and the presence of diabetes.
2.  **Hyperkalemia:** Risk from lisinopril use, which can be exacerbated by any degree of renal impairment or diabetes.
3.  **Lactic Acidosis:** Risk from metformin use if renal function is impaired (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² is a contraindication; dose adjustment is required starting at eGFR <45).
4.  **Contrast-Induced Nephropathy:** If the patient requires imaging with iodinated contrast, metformin may need to be withheld, depending on renal function, to prevent this complication and subsequent lactic acidosis.
5.  **Hepatotoxicity:** Diclofenac metabolism can be altered in hepatic impairment, increasing toxicity risk. Hepatic function is currently unknown.
