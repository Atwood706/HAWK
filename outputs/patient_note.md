### **Your Medication Safety Review: Important Information for You**

**For:** 55-year-old patient with High Blood Pressure and Type 2 Diabetes.
**Your Current Medications:** Metformin (for diabetes), Lisinopril (for blood pressure), Diclofenac (a prescription pain reliever/anti-inflammatory).

**Summary for You:**
We reviewed your medications and found an important safety concern. Taking your blood pressure medicine (Lisinopril) together with your pain medicine (Diclofenac) can be hard on your kidneys. This is especially important because having diabetes can also affect kidney health. If the kidneys don't work as well, it can cause other problems with your other medications.

**Key Points for You to Know:**

1.  **Kidney Safety:** The main concern is that the combination of Lisinopril and Diclofenac can increase the risk of sudden kidney problems.
2.  **Other Medication Effects:** If kidney function changes, it can affect how your body handles the other medicines, potentially leading to issues with high potassium levels or other side effects.
3.  **Missing Information:** We don't have your latest kidney or liver blood test results, which are needed to manage your medications safely.

---

### **What You Should Do:**

**DO:**
*   **Get Lab Tests Done:** It is very important to get blood tests **before you continue taking these medications together**. The tests needed are:
    *   A **Basic Metabolic Panel** (checks kidney function and potassium).
    *   A **Liver Function Test**.
*   **Stay Hydrated:** Drink plenty of fluids, especially if you are sick, have diarrhea, or are sweating a lot.
*   **Inform All Healthcare Providers:** Always tell every doctor, dentist, or radiologist that you take **Metformin**, especially before any scan or procedure that might use "contrast dye."
*   **Talk to Your Doctor:** Discuss the reason you are taking Diclofenac (the pain medicine) and how long you need to take it. There may be safer alternatives.

**DON'T:**
*   **Do Not Use Other Similar Pain Relievers:** Avoid over-the-counter pain medicines like **ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin), naproxen (Aleve), or aspirin** unless your doctor specifically says it's okay. These are in the same drug family as Diclofenac and carry the same risks.
*   **Do Not Use Potassium Supplements or Salt Substitutes:** Avoid these unless your doctor has prescribed them, as they can increase potassium levels when taking Lisinopril.

---

### **When to Seek URGENT Medical Care:**
Contact your doctor **immediately** or go to an urgent care clinic/emergency room if you experience any of the following symptoms:

*   **Signs of Kidney Problems:** A large decrease in how much you urinate, swelling in your feet/ankles, or unusual shortness of breath.
*   **Signs of High Potassium:** New or sudden muscle weakness, numbness, tingling, or feeling like your heart is pounding, fluttering, or skipping beats.
*   **Signs of Severe Side Effects:** Severe stomach pain, unusual tiredness/dizziness, or feeling very cold.

### **Next Steps with Your Doctor:**
1.  Get the recommended blood tests as soon as possible.
2.  Schedule a follow-up appointment with your doctor to review the test results and discuss the plan for your pain medicine.
3.  If you and your doctor decide to continue all your current medicines, you will need to have your kidney function and potassium checked again in **1-2 weeks**, and then regularly after that.

**Please Note:** This information is to help you understand your medication safety and guide your conversation with your doctor. It is not a replacement for your doctor's professional medical advice. Your doctor will make the final decisions about your care based on your full health picture.
