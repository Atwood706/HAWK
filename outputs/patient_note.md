### **Your Health Summary & Important Next Steps**

**About You:** This summary is for a 55-year-old person being treated for high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes. Your current medicines are listed as:
*   Metformin (500 mg, twice a day) for diabetes.
*   Lisinopril (10 mg, once a day) for blood pressure.
*   Diclofenac (75 mg, twice a day), which is a strong pain reliever similar to ibuprofen.

**Important Safety Notes for Your Care Team:**
Your care team needs more information to make sure your current medicines are safe for you. The combination of your blood pressure medicine (lisinopril) and the pain reliever (diclofenac) can sometimes cause problems for the kidneys. Also, we need to check your kidney and liver health to guide the safe use of your diabetes medicine (metformin) and the pain reliever.

---

### **What You Can Do & What to Watch For**

**Do:**
1.  **Get Lab Tests Soon:** It is very important to get the following blood tests as soon as possible to check your kidney and liver health:
    *   Kidney function test (includes creatinine and eGFR).
    *   Basic metabolic panel (checks potassium and other salts).
    *   Liver function test (checks for liver health).
2.  **Be Prepared to Answer These Questions for Your Doctor:**
    *   Why are you taking diclofenac? (e.g., for arthritis, back pain?)
    *   Do you have any imaging tests (like CT scans with "IV contrast dye") scheduled?
    *   What are **all** the medicines, vitamins, herbs, and over-the-counter drugs (like aspirin, ibuprofen, or naproxen) you take?
    *   Do you have any drug allergies, especially to iodine or contrast dye?
3.  **Talk to Your Doctor About Pain Management:** Discuss if there are other ways to manage your pain that might be safer for your kidneys than long-term use of diclofenac.

**Don't:**
*   Do **NOT** take any other over-the-counter pain medicines like ibuprofen (Advil®, Motrin®), naproxen (Aleve®), or aspirin unless your doctor specifically tells you it's okay.
*   Do **NOT** forget to tell every healthcare provider you see about **all** the medicines you take, especially before any X-ray or scan.

---

### **When to Seek Urgent Care**
**Contact your doctor immediately or go to an urgent care clinic/emergency room if you experience any of the following:**

*   **Signs of Kidney Problems:** A big decrease in how much you urinate, new or worsening swelling in your feet, ankles, or legs, or unusual shortness of breath.
*   **Signs of High Potassium (Hyperkalemia):** New or sudden muscle weakness, numbness, tingling, or feeling like your heart is pounding, fluttering, or skipping beats (palpitations).
*   **Signs of Liver Problems:** Severe tiredness, unusual nausea, pain in the upper right side of your belly, yellowing of your skin or the whites of your eyes (jaundice), or dark urine.
*   **If You Have a Planned Procedure:** If you are scheduled for a scan that uses "IV contrast dye," you must talk to your doctor about when to safely pause your metformin before and after the test.

---

### **Ongoing Health Management**
*   **Regular Monitoring:** You will need regular blood tests to monitor your kidney function, potassium levels, and liver health, especially while taking your current medicines.
*   **Routine Checks:** Keep up with your usual monitoring of blood pressure and blood sugar (A1c) levels.
