**Your Medication & Health Summary**

We have reviewed your current medications and health history. Here is what we found and what you need to know to stay safe.

**Your Current Medications:**
- Metformin (for diabetes) – 500 mg, twice a day
- Lisinopril (for blood pressure) – 10 mg, once a day
- Diclofenac (for pain) – 75 mg, twice a day

**The Main Concern:**
Taking **Lisinopril** (a blood pressure pill) and **Diclofenac** (a pain reliever like ibuprofen or Aleve) together can sometimes cause problems. This combination can:
- Make your blood pressure medicine less effective.
- Increase your risk of **kidney injury**.

Because of this, it is very important to check your kidney function and blood pressure soon.

**What We Need to Do Next (Tests & Questions):**
1.  **Blood Test:** We need to check your kidney function (creatinine and eGFR) right away. This is the most important step.
2.  **Blood Pressure Check:** We need to know your current blood pressure and heart rate.
3.  **Questions for You:**
    - Have you noticed any decrease in urination, or any swelling in your legs or ankles?
    - Have you felt dizzy, lightheaded, or fainted recently?
    - Are you taking any other medications, especially one called **tizanidine** (a muscle relaxant)?
    - Why are you taking Diclofenac? Is it for a specific injury or ongoing pain?

**What You Should Do:**

**DO:**
- Take your medications as prescribed until you speak with your doctor.
- Tell your doctor if you have any new swelling, dizziness, or changes in urination.
- Ask your doctor if a different pain reliever (like acetaminophen/Tylenol) might be safer for you.

**DON'T:**
- Do not stop or change your medications without talking to your doctor first.
- Do not take extra doses of Diclofenac or any other NSAID (like ibuprofen, naproxen, or Aleve) without checking with your doctor.

**When to Seek Urgent Care:**
Call your doctor or go to the nearest emergency room right away if you experience:
- A big decrease in how much you urinate (or no urination at all)
- New or worsening swelling in your legs, ankles, or feet
- Severe dizziness or fainting
- Shortness of breath
